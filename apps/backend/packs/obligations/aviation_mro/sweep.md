# Where MRO obligations hide

- **Price and scope are in different exhibits.** A rate exhibit gives the number; a workscope
  exhibit says what it covers; an exclusions list says what escapes it. A rate record extracted
  without its exclusions overstates what is fixed.
- **The exclusions list is where the money is.** Beyond-economic-repair, corrosion, foreign object
  damage, life-limited parts and prior-repair findings each convert a fixed price into a
  time-and-material charge. Each is an obligation with a consequence.
- **Turn times are tabulated by check or component class**, and the clock's start event is stated
  separately from the duration — induction, receipt of the unit, or receipt of the customer's
  approval. Capture the event as well as the number.
- **AOG terms sit apart from ordinary turn times**, with their own tiers, premiums and escalation
  contacts.
- **Findings duties carry two numbers**: a money threshold above which approval is required, and a
  window in which the supplier must report. Both are easy to lose in a long prose paragraph.
- **Client duties cluster in a "customer obligations" or "customer furnished equipment" article**
  and in the delivery-condition annex. They are obligations, owed the other way.
- **Warranty has its own notice deadline**, separate from the general notice article, and its own
  exclusions.
- **Rate escalation is a separate clause** governing every rate in every exhibit.
- **Governing approvals appear as standards, not duties** — a paragraph naming an aviation authority
  approval or a quality standard usually creates a continuing obligation to hold it.
